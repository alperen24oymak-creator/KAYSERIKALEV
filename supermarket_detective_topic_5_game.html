🔢 1. COUNTABLE NOUNS (We CAN count: 1, 2, 3...)

They have a Singular (1) and a Plural (2+ with -s) form!

🍎 Singular (1): an apple, a banana, a carrot, an egg, a cookie
🍎🍎 Plural (2+): two apples, three bananas, carrots, eggs

💧 2. UNCOUNTABLE NOUNS (We CANNOT count: 1, 2, 3)

Liquids, powders, & soft foods. ALWAYS Singular! NO "-s"! NO "a/an"!

🥛 Examples: water, milk, juice, sugar, salt, flour, rice, cheese, bread, meat, butter, honey
❌ Never say: "a milk", "two waters", "three breads"
Grammar Word Countable OR Uncountable? Simple Rule Easy Example
There is Singular (1) & Uncountable For 1 thing OR uncountable There is a banana. / There is some milk.
There are Plural Countable (2+) ONLY for 2 or more things (-s) There are three apples.
afew (birkaç) 🔢 Plural Countable (-s) Small number (We CAN count) I have a few cookies. 🍪🍪
a little (biraz) 💧 Uncountable Small amount (CANNOT count) There is a little water / sugar. 💧
some (biraz / birkaç) ✅ BOTH (Plural & Uncount.) Used in Positive (+) sentences There are some eggs. / There is some cheese.
any (hiç) ✅ BOTH (Plural & Uncount.) Used in Negative (-) & Questions (?) There isn't any milk. / Are there any apples?
a lot of (çok) ✅ BOTH (Plural & Uncount.) Big amount or big number! There are a lot of chips / a lot of water.
How many / many 🔢 Plural Countable (-s) Ask/talk about number (with -s) How many oranges are there? Not many!
How much / much 💧 Uncountable Ask/talk about amount (no -s) How much milk is there? Not much!

📖 Quick Grammar Cheat Sheet

🔢 COUNTABLE (1, 2, 3 + s)

• Singular: There is a/an apple.

• Plural: There are apples.

• Small number: a few apples

• Question: How many apples?

💧 UNCOUNTABLE (NO -s, NO a/an)

• Always Singular: There is milk.

• Small amount: a little milk

• Question: How much milk?

• Examples: water, sugar, bread, cheese, rice, meat

✨ BOTH COUNTABLE & UNCOUNTABLE:

• some: Positive (+) sentences ➡️ "There is some water / some cookies."

• any: Negative (-) & Questions (?) ➡️ "There isn't any water. Are there any cookies?"

• a lot of: Big amount/number ➡️ "a lot of apples / a lot of milk"
